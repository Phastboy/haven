# v0.4.x Untangling and Planning: Inquiry

This document untangles the product roadmap items for **v0.4.x ("Can someone act on it?")** following the [`haven-journey`](../expts/rust/.agents/skills/haven-journey/SKILL.md) and [`haven-product`](../expts/rust/.agents/skills/haven-product/SKILL.md) guidelines.

**What changed from the first draft.** The first draft modelled a `commitment` per `(offer, responder)` with a status lifecycle. That was dropped for three reasons:

- Nothing here is binding until the 1.0.x agreement, so "commitment" was the wrong word. This stage is an **inquiry**.
- Status and tracking (`Accepted`, `Declined`, `Withdrawn`) are consequences, and consequences belong to 1.0.x.
- A thread per offer loses the relationship when the offer is deleted or sold, and Haven's goal is people coming back to a maker.

The model is now: **one 1:1 conversation per pair of people, with messages that can be tagged with an offer.**

---

## 1. Roadmap Stage Alignment

- **Stage**: `0.4.x — Can someone act on it?`
- **Core question**: *Can someone do something meaningful about an offer?*
- **Done when**: *An offer can receive a meaningful response from another person, and the system meets its load-testing performance contract.*

How each `ROADMAP.md` checkbox is answered:

| Checkbox | Answered by |
|---|---|
| A person can express their willingness to take on an offer. | A person starts (or continues) a conversation from an offer; the first message is tagged with that offer. |
| The person who made the offer can know about that expression of interest, and who it came from. | An unread indicator in the profile shell (0.4.0), and a per-offer "who asked" view derived from tagged messages (0.4.1). |
| People can communicate when communication is necessary to understand or progress that inquiry. | The 1:1 conversation itself. |

---

## 2. Decisions (settled)

| # | Decision | Outcome | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name | **Inquiry**, not commitment | Nothing is binding until 1.0.x. |
| 2 | Communication | **Private messaging inside Haven**; no external-platform fallback in 0.4.x | Keeps the relationship and its context on the platform, which 1.0.x needs. |
| 3 | Status / tracking | **None** | Consequences belong to 1.0.x. Interest is just messages. |
| 4 | Conversation shape | **Exactly one 1:1 conversation per pair of people**, any number of offer tags inside it | Relationship outlives any single offer. |
| 5 | Capacity | Offers are never locked or reserved | A lock is an agreement mechanism (1.0.x). |
| 6 | Notification | In-app unread indicator first (0.4.0); email later (0.4.2) | Email is an infrastructure decision, not required by checkbox 2. |
| 7 | Delivery to an open page | **Realtime in 0.4.0**: server-sent events push incoming messages to the open thread; sending stays a plain `POST` | A conversation should feel live; refreshing or waiting on a timer loses that. Realtime does not replace the unread indicator or email, which reach people who are not on the page. |

## 4. Sub-Journeys

### Sub-Journey 1: Ask about an offer (0.4.0)

- **Goal**: A person who sees an offer they want can reach its maker with a clear reason to talk.
- **Preconditions**: The offer is public. The person is signed in (otherwise sign-in preserves the return destination and intent).
- **Steps**:
  1. Person visits `/offers/{slug}`.
  2. The action area shows **"Message about this offer"**. It is not shown on the person's own offers.
  3. A short form asks for the first message (required, max 2,000 characters).
  4. `POST /offers/{slug}/message` gets or creates the conversation for the pair, appends the message tagged with the offer, and redirects to `/conversations/{id}`.
- **Edge conditions & invariants**:
  - **Self-message**: rejected in the domain, and the button is suppressed in the UI.
  - **Existing conversation**: the same endpoint appends to it. There is never a second conversation for the same pair.
  - **Offer deleted or hidden mid-submit**: the request fails with an honest gone notice and no message is created.
  - **Double submit**: a duplicate within a short window (same sender, same body, same conversation) is ignored via an idempotency token on the form.
  - **Spam**: a conversation can only be *started* from someone's public offer, never from a bare profile (see section 7).

### Sub-Journey 2: Maker finds out (0.4.0 for the indicator and inbox, 0.4.1 for the per-offer view)

- **Goal**: The maker learns someone reached out, who it was, and which offer it was about.
- **Steps**:
  1. The profile shell shows an unread indicator whenever any conversation has a message from the other person newer than the viewer's last read.
  2. `/conversations` lists conversations, most recently active first, with the other person's avatar (fallback initials), name, `@handle`, a message preview, and an unread marker.
  3. `/conversations/{id}` shows the full exchange. A tagged message renders a compact offer reference (cover image, title, link to the public slug).
  4. *(0.4.1)* `/profile/offers/{id}` shows **"N people asked about this"**, derived from messages tagged with that offer and grouped by sender, each linking to their conversation.
- **Edge conditions & invariants**:
  - **Empty state**: calm, honest copy for no conversations, and for an offer nobody has asked about.
  - **Privacy between askers**: conversations are strictly 1:1, so one asker can never see another.
  - **Tag to a deleted or hidden offer**: renders "This offer is no longer available" in place of the reference. The message itself stays.

### Sub-Journey 3: Talk (0.4.0)

- **Goal**: Both people can clarify whatever they need to.
- **Steps**:
  1. Either participant opens `/conversations/{id}`.
  2. The page shows the other person's identity, messages in chronological order attributed by sender with relative timestamps, and a composer.
  3. `POST /conversations/{id}/messages` appends the message and redirects back. Opening the conversation marks it read for the viewer.
  4. A message may carry an offer tag, and tagging works the same way for both people:
     - **Someone else's offer**: tagged by arriving from that offer's page ("Message about this offer"), as in Sub-Journey 1.
     - **Your own offer**: the composer has an optional "Attach one of your offers" `<select>` listing the sender's own public offers (a native control, so it works with zero JS). This is how a maker says "this other one might suit you", and how an asker can mention something of theirs.
     - A message carries at most one tag.
- **Edge conditions & invariants**:
  - **Strict IDOR protection**: only the two participants can read or post. Everyone else gets an indistinguishable 404.
  - **Validation**: blank or over-length messages are rejected with inline errors.
  - **Tags**: only currently public offers can be tagged, and the offer must belong to either participant. The server checks this on every send; a forged `offer_id` for anyone else's offer is rejected.
  - **Long select**: a maker with many offers gets a long list. Cap the select at the most recent offers for 0.4.0, and revisit with a search-as-you-type picker only if real use shows the need.
  - **Zero-JS**: the whole flow works with plain form `POST` and redirects on Topcoat server rendering. Realtime is layered on top: with JS off, the thread simply shows new messages on the next load.
  - **Realtime**: the open thread subscribes to `/conversations/{id}/events` and appends incoming messages without a reload. The sender's own message appears through the normal redirect (or the same stream), never twice. If the stream drops, the browser reconnects and catches up from the last message id.
  - **One thread view for both people**: the page differs only in who "the other person" is. Do not build two near-identical pages.

### Sub-Journey 4: Email nudge (0.4.2)

- **Goal**: A maker who rarely signs in still finds out.
- **Rule**: at most one email per conversation until the recipient next reads it, so a burst of messages produces one email, not ten.
- **Dependency**: real mail transport (the experiment's `FileTransport` is not enough). This is why it is its own release.

## 5. Domain & Data Architecture

### Domain (`haven-domain`)

```rust
/// Two distinct people, stored in a canonical order so that
/// (a, b) and (b, a) are the same pair. The only constructor
/// enforces `low < high`, so an unordered or self pair cannot exist.
pub struct ConversationPair {
    low: UserId,
    high: UserId,
}

impl ConversationPair {
    /// Returns an error if both ids are equal.
    pub fn new(a: UserId, b: UserId) -> Result<Self, SelfConversation>;
    pub fn other(&self, me: UserId) -> Option<UserId>;
}

pub struct Conversation {
    pub id: ConversationId,
    pub pair: ConversationPair,
    pub created_at: DateTime<Utc>,
    pub last_message_at: DateTime<Utc>,
}

pub struct Message {
    pub id: MessageId,
    pub conversation_id: ConversationId,
    pub sender_id: UserId,
    pub body: MessageBody,          // validated: non-blank, <= 2,000 chars
    pub offer_id: Option<OfferId>,  // the tag
    pub created_at: DateTime<Utc>,
}

/// Grouped input, so the repository call stays under three arguments.
pub struct NewMessage {
    pub sender_id: UserId,
    pub body: MessageBody,
    pub offer_id: Option<OfferId>,
}
```

Ports: a `ConversationRepository` with `get_or_create(pair)`, `append(conversation_id, NewMessage)`, `list_for_user(user, page)`, `messages(conversation_id, page)`, `mark_read(conversation_id, user)`, `unread_exists(user)`. A hermetic fake implements the same trait.

### Relational schema (`migrations/<next>_conversations_and_messages.sql`)

```sql
CREATE TABLE conversation (
    id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    user_low        UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES "user"(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    user_high       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES "user"(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    created_at      TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW(),
    last_message_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW(),
    CONSTRAINT ck_conversation_pair_ordered CHECK (user_low < user_high),
    CONSTRAINT uq_conversation_pair UNIQUE (user_low, user_high)
);

-- Read state lives with each participant. This is deliberately not a status.
CREATE TABLE conversation_participant (
    conversation_id UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES conversation(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    user_id         UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES "user"(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    last_read_at    TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW(),
    PRIMARY KEY (conversation_id, user_id)
);
CREATE INDEX idx_participant_user ON conversation_participant(user_id);

CREATE TABLE message (
    id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    conversation_id UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES conversation(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    sender_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES "user"(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    body            VARCHAR(2000) NOT NULL CHECK (length(btrim(body)) > 0),
    offer_id        UUID REFERENCES offer(id) ON DELETE SET NULL,
    created_at      TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW()
);

CREATE INDEX idx_message_thread ON message(conversation_id, created_at, id);
-- Powers the 0.4.1 "who asked about this offer" view.
CREATE INDEX idx_message_offer ON message(offer_id, created_at DESC) WHERE offer_id IS NOT NULL;
```

Notes:

- **Get-or-create** is `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (user_low, user_high) DO NOTHING` followed by a select, in the same transaction that inserts both participant rows. Two people messaging each other at the same moment converge on one conversation.
- **Unread** for a user in a conversation means `last_message_at > last_read_at` and the latest message is not their own. Sending a message also sets the sender's `last_read_at`, so people never see their own message as unread.
- **`ON DELETE SET NULL` on the tag** keeps the message when an offer is deleted. The UI shows "no longer available".
- **Why a participant table rather than two columns on `conversation`**: "my conversations, newest first" becomes one clean indexed lookup instead of an `OR` across two columns. Confirm with the k6 run.

### Routes (`haven-web`)

| Route | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /offers/{slug}/message` | Start or continue a conversation from an offer | Slug is the public path; auth required; not own offer |
| `GET /conversations` | Inbox | Keyset-paginated |
| `GET /conversations/{id}` | Conversation view; marks read | Participants only, else 404 |
| `POST /conversations/{id}/messages` | Send a message | Participants only; idempotency token |
| `GET /conversations/{id}/events` | Server-sent event stream of new messages for the open thread | Participants only, else 404; `Last-Event-ID` is the last message id seen, so a reconnect catches up from the database; heartbeat comment every ~20s; capped streams per user; closes when the session ends |
| `GET /profile/offers/{id}` *(0.4.1)* | Owner view gains "who asked" | Owner path, ownership checked by ID |

## 6. Implementation Phases

### 0.4.0 Start a conversation, talk, and know something arrived
1. **Domain** (`haven-domain`): `ConversationPair`, `Conversation`, `Message`, `MessageBody`, `NewMessage`, repository port and fake.
2. **Persistence** (`haven-db`): migration above, get-or-create, append, list, mark read, unread check; `sqlx::test` coverage including the concurrent-first-message case.
3. **Delivery** (`haven-web`): the routes above except the per-offer view, including the events stream; authorization helpers shared by every conversation route; a message broadcaster port with an in-process implementation, called after a message is committed.
4. **UI**: "Message about this offer" action component; composer with the optional "attach one of your offers" select; inbox row (avatar or initials, name, preview, unread marker); thread view; unread indicator in the profile shell. Each is its own component, so improving one never means editing the page assembly.
5. **Verification**: k6 scenario `05-messaging` for the request mix, plus a connection-capacity scenario that holds open streams while messages are sent (k6 may need an extension for server-sent events; check before writing it). Run it locally with the server and k6 pinned to separate cores and the open-file limit raised above the stream count. Security review pass: IDOR (including on the stream), CSRF, XSS in message bodies and in appended fragments, rate limiting, idempotency, the per-user stream cap, and the stream closing when the session ends.

### 0.4.1 See who asked about an offer
- Per-offer "who asked" query over `idx_message_offer`, keyset-paginated, rendered on the owner's offer page.
- Owner-side empty state.
- Re-run the load contract with this query in the mix.

### 0.4.2 Email notification
- Real mail transport, throttle rule from Sub-Journey 4, unsubscribe/preferences surface decided before shipping.
- Load and abuse review of the notification trigger path.

## 7. Risks Accepted in This Design

- **No block or report yet.** Mitigations in 0.4.0: a conversation can only be started from a public offer, never cold from a profile; per-user rate limits on new conversations and on messages; idempotency on submit. Block and report are deliberately outside this stage, but they should be a conscious decision before Haven is opened to people beyond a trusted group.
- **No read receipts or typing state.** `last_read_at` is private to its owner and is used only for the unread indicator.
- **Realtime adds held connections.** Mitigations: a cap on open streams per user, a heartbeat so idle proxies do not cut streams silently, nginx streaming settings (response buffering off, a long read timeout) on the capacity-test VM, and a separate connection-capacity gate next to the RPS contract. The broadcaster sits behind a port with an in-process implementation, which is enough for a single instance. A `LISTEN/NOTIFY` version could replace it later without touching handlers, but it keeps a database connection open for as long as the app runs, which matters on a database with limited active time.
- **Test environment is not the deployment.** Load tests run on the developer PC (the VM was removed for storage space), while the target is a free Koyeb instance. The Koyeb docs reviewed list it as 0.1 vCPU and 512 MB RAM, one free web service, sleeping after an hour without traffic, and do not confirm horizontal scaling on the free tier, so a single instance is assumed. PC results are for catching regressions and comparing runs, not for predicting production numbers. A sleeping instance drops its streams; reconnecting with catch-up from the last message id covers that. Re-check Koyeb's current limits before relying on any of this.
- **Unbounded conversation growth.** Handled by keyset pagination on both the inbox and the thread, so cost does not grow with history.

## 8. Open Questions

1. **Realtime capacity target.** Pick how many concurrently open threads must be held alongside the 1,000 RPS contract. Proposed starting point: 1,000 held streams for 5 minutes while messages are being sent, adjusted after measuring memory per open stream, because the deployment target is a small instance (see section 7). The deployment is a single instance, so the in-process broadcaster is enough for 0.4.0 and no `LISTEN/NOTIFY` implementation is needed.
2. **Load-test mix for the 1,000 RPS contract.** Proposal to confirm: read-heavy, with most requests on thread view, some on inbox, and a small share on sending messages. Settle the exact ratios before `05-messaging` is written.

## 9. Explicit Scope Boundaries

### In scope for v0.4.x
- Starting a private 1:1 conversation from a public offer.
- Messages in that conversation, optionally tagged with an offer.
- The maker (and any participant) knowing something new arrived, and from whom.
- A per-offer "who asked about this" view.
- Email notification (0.4.2).
- Keyset pagination of the inbox and threads.

### Explicitly out of scope
- Any status, accept/decline, reservation, or tracking of an inquiry (the agreement stage, 1.0.x).
- Group conversations, public threaded comments on offers, attachments, reactions.
- Starting a conversation from a bare profile.
- **v0.5.x**: search and filtering. **v0.6.x**: saving offers, following makers. **v0.7.x**: category and tag filters. **v1.0.x**: formal agreement, holding, closing. **2.0.x**: payments, disputes, fulfilment.
